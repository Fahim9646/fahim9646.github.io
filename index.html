Hello, I am Fahim. I am a civil engineer from the Rajshahi University of Engineering and Technology (RUET), working between experimental soil mechanics and computational modelling. My research interests lie in geotechnical engineering, in particular the discrete element modelling of granular soils, inherent anisotropy and soil-structure interfaces, and in sustainable construction in the context of Bangladesh.

My undergraduate thesis at RUET, supervised by Prof. Md. Mahmud Sazzad, is a matched experimental and DEM study of how bedding-plane anisotropy carries over from sand to sand-concrete interfaces. I built the DEM engine from scratch and calibrated it against our direct shear tests. The resulting journal manuscript is in preparation, and I am extending the work to sand-pile interfaces under lateral loading.

I currently work as an Executive, Quality Control (on-site) at Rancon Engineering & Consultants Ltd., with the Project Management Office on site quality, schedules, documentation and reporting. Before that, I was a Planning Engineer in the Civil Engineering Department of Mir Cement Ltd. (Narayanganj Factory), handling project scheduling, cost control and daily quality inspections.

I plan to continue with graduate research (MSc/PhD) and, in the long run, move into academia. I am happy to hear from prospective supervisors and collaborators.

Hindrances in Constructing Green Buildings in Bangladesh: A Comprehensive Study

7th International Conference on Civil Engineering for Sustainable Development (ICCESD 2024), KUET, Khulna · with Sifat Shahriar

Why it matters. The construction sector uses up to 40% of global energy, and buildings account for roughly 32% of energy use in Bangladesh. Green buildings are a response, yet the country has no specific guideline for them and developers mostly seek foreign (LEED) certification. This paper asks which barriers matter most.

Method. A 10-question survey (Google Forms) was sent to 160 experts and members of the public; 87 responses came back. The answers were grouped into seven criteria and ranked with the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), using pairwise comparisons on a 1–9 scale in AHP-OS. The comparison was consistent (λmax = 7.087, consistency ratio = 0.011, well under the 0.10 limit).

Findings. Lack of government involvement ranked first (25.0%), followed by ownership of residence (23.8%, since many people rent) and technical difficulties (18.7%, including scarce skilled manpower and materials). Willingness to convert and overall expense tied for fourth (10.4% each); indifference (6.0%) and satisfaction with current bills (5.7%) mattered least.

Recommendations. New legislation for green practices in new construction, government certification for developers, yearly awareness programmes and loans for homeowners, and green construction in vocational and engineering education.

Transmission and Attenuation of Bedding-Plane Anisotropy from Sand to Sand-Concrete Interfaces: A Discrete Element Study

Md. Abdullah Al-Fahim, Md. Mahmud Sazzad (supervisor), M. S. M. Shafi · Department of Civil Engineering, RUET

Aim: to test how the inherent (bedding-plane) anisotropy of sand is transmitted from bulk sand to a sand–concrete interface, using matched laboratory direct shear tests and a two-dimensional discrete element model.

What I developed

  • A custom two-dimensional DEM engine written from scratch in Python/PyTorch (float64), with GPU acceleration.
  • Grain generator for clumped particles: three-disk clumps for sub-rounded river sand and four-disk clumps for angular crushed sand, with sizes drawn from the sieve curve and each grain tilted to the target bedding angle with small random scatter.
  • A matched laboratory programme of 48 direct shear tests (ASTM D3080) on the two sands, sand–sand and sand–concrete, at four bedding angles and three normal stresses.
  • Post-processing for peak friction angle, stress–strain response and three fabric tensors (contact normals, force-weighted contact normals, grain long-axis orientation).

What the code does

  • Contact model: at every grain–grain and grain–wall contact, a linear spring–dashpot acts in the normal direction (push-only, no tension), an elastic–perfectly plastic spring acts in the tangential direction (capped by Coulomb friction), and an elastic–perfectly plastic rolling-resistance spring resists relative grain rotation.
  • Specimen preparation: grains are dropped under gravity and settled, then consolidated to the target normal stress by a rigid load plate whose height is adjusted each step from the measured plate force.
  • Sand–sand shear: the specimen is split into a driven upper raft, a fixed base and a free band around the shear plane, so the shear zone develops from the contact laws alone.
  • Sand–concrete interface: the concrete surface is modelled as a sawtooth wall scaled to grain size, with contact forces tilted to whichever tooth face a grain rests against; the sand block is then displaced over it in small drive-and-relax cycles.
  • Calibrate-once protocol: friction and rolling-resistance parameters are tuned against experiment at a single bedding angle and then frozen, so every other bedding angle is a blind prediction; each condition is repeated with three random seeds.
  • Specimen-size study: the same model is run at two specimen lengths to test whether the bedding-angle signal survives in direct shear.

Keywords: discrete element method; inherent anisotropy; bedding angle; sand-concrete interface; direct shear; interface roughness

    Md. Abdullah Al-Fahim and Sifat Shahriar
    In Proceedings of the 7th International Conference on Civil Engineering for Sustainable Development (ICCESD 2024), KUET, Khulna, Bangladesh, Feb 2024
    Full paper, Paper ID 710
    Abs Bib PDF

    Bangladesh, a developing country in Southeast Asia with a comparatively small contribution to climate change, is nonetheless susceptible to its adversities. This study presents a comprehensive analysis of the challenges that arise when constructing green buildings in such a setting. The research combined qualitative and quantitative methods: 160 questionnaires were distributed to experts and the general public, and 87 responses were obtained and analysed with the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP). The data show that public awareness, the inability of the masses to cover the high initial cost, and the lack of individual as well as organisational initiative are noteworthy hindrances. Government involvement ranked first among seven criteria (25.0%), followed by ownership of residence (23.8%) and technical difficulties (18.7%). The study offers recommendations for policymakers, stakeholders and organisations.

    Transmission and Attenuation of Bedding-Plane Anisotropy from Sand to Sand-Concrete Interfaces: A Discrete Element Study
    Md. Abdullah Al-Fahim, Md. Mahmud Sazzad, and M. S. M. Shafi
    2026
    In preparation
    Abs Bib

    Naturally deposited sands are inherently anisotropic, and their peak friction depends on the orientation of the sedimentary bedding plane relative to the shear direction. This study reports a matched experimental and two-dimensional DEM investigation of two sands of contrasting angularity (sub-rounded river sand, angular crushed sand) sheared against themselves and against a concrete surface of intermediate roughness at four bedding angles and three normal stresses. Contact friction and rolling resistance are calibrated once, at θ = 0°, and then frozen, so the response at θ = 15°, 30° and 45° is a genuinely blind prediction. Results are withheld until the manuscript is published.
